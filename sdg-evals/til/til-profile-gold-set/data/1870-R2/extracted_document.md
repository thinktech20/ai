TECHNICAL INFORMATION LETTER
FIRST ROW COMPRESSOR BLADE STAKING INSPECTIONS
APPLICATION
The recommended inspection should be completed on F-class units that received an RO re-installation from January
2008 to November 2014.
PURPOSE
To inform sites that there is a potential for first row blade migration caused by insufficient interference between the
stake marks and the blade. During annual borescope inspections, it is recommended to confirm ROs have not migrated
forward. The staking quality can be verified during a major inspection or when the bellmouth is scheduled to be
removed.
REASON FOR REVISION
Expanded affected unit list and updated recommendations to reflect the more robust field installation procedural
changes.

BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
GE compressor blades are retained in the wheel by the method commonly referred to as staking. Staking peens wheel
material over a portion of the blade dovetail to prevent forward migration of the blade. GE has used this method of
airfoil retention since the late 1940s. F-class R0 blades are held axially into the forward stub shaft by two peen marks at
the base of the dovetail on the forward and aft side of the wheel dovetail slot. The peening marks displace material in
the dovetail slot bottom and create a small tab over the base of the blade, or interference, which prevents the blade
from moving axially forward. After multiple blade installations, there is no longer enough room on the slot bottom edge
for staking, and an R0 staking replaceable insert (biscuit) is inserted to perform the same function as the slot bottom
edge (see Figure 1).
Figure 1: Staking with biscuit mod
There has been a small population of turbines that have had migration events related to improper blade installation,
with installations dating back to 2008. Inside the small population of migration events, the majority of migrations have
occurred in North American units, and some soon after installation, that had RO re-installations performed by a specific
team. The recommendations for this population are outlinedin TIL 1796. Recently, there have been a few occurrences
of blade migration outside of North America that have had a greater duration of time between installation and event.
This TIL provides recommendations for all other RO blade re-installations since 2008.
Blade migration can occur when there is insufficient interference between the displaced material from staking and the
blade dovetail, allowing axial movement during operation. Units that experienced blade migration had relatively large
radial gaps between the bottom of the blade dovetail and the wheel slot bottoms and/or stake marks that did not meet
installation requirements. During operation, the blades are loaded into the wheel dovetails and axial movement is
unlikely; however, at slower speeds, axial blade movement is possible. The axial movement could eventually lead to
compressor blades rubbing against the casing bellmouth, causing damage to the blade and rub ring. RO migration has
occurred in units with and without enhanced blades, and with and without a staking insert modification.

The depth, diameter, and height of the peen marks are closely controlled by the RO blade installation procedure. The
procedure also specifies and controls the minimum radial gap between the bottom of the blade and the wheel slot
bottom. Additionally, the procedure calls for the biscuits to be staked such that they are in full engagement with the RO
blade dovetails. These requirements should be met to ensure the displaced material's interference with the blade is
sufficient to prevent the RO from migrating forward.
RECOMMENDATIONS
NOTE: Follow standard and site-specific safety procedures and any required lock-out/tag-out (LOTO) procedures when
performing any work for this TIL. Customers should refer to the GE Operation and Maintenance Manual for any additional
recommendations or safety warnings specific to this equipment.
It is recommended for units with R0 re-installations between January 2008 and November 2014 to perform the
following inspections:
1. Visually inspect R0s for axial migration.
During the annual borescope inspection, confirm that no RO blades have migrated forward. Migration can be
detected via a visual inspection or with the aid of a borescope. See Figure 3 for an example of a migrated blade
observed with a borescope.
Figure 3: Migration with bellmouth rub
These inspections will help confirm that the RO blades are not rubbing into the rub ring during operation. Any
blades that move axially forward will need to be re-staked to ensure the staking ligament is actively engaged
with the blade. Please contact your GE representative for further recommendations.
2. Inspect for staking insert rotation, if applicable.
During the annual borescope, confirm that staking inserts (if applicable) have not significantly rotated. Rotation
can be detected via a visual inspection; the aid of a mirror may be necessary. See Figure 4 for an example of a
rotated insert detected with the aid of a mirror. Significant insert rotation may indicate that one of the stake
marks has moved under the RO, and the quality of the second stake mark cannot be confirmed. In general, if
the stake marks are forward of the blade, the stake marks are still able to provide interference and axially
retain the RO (see Figure 5).

It is possible there will be some staking insert rotation, since the staking insert is a round element in a round
pocket. The staking interference should limit rotation of the staking insert and prevent blade migration. A small
amount of rotation is acceptable for continued normal operation. It can be challenging to determine if the
stake marks are forward of the blade during a borescope inspection. The stake marks can be hidden by the
chamfer of the blade, the blade can be sitting further aft in the slot allowing for additional rotation, or the insert
pocket could have been drilled slightly forward allowing for additional rotation. These conditions are
acceptable as long as both stake marks are forward of and retaining the blades. Figure 5 shows an axial view
of two biscuits rotated a similar amount; however, in one case both stake marks are in front of the blade, and in
the other, one stake mark is under the blade. If mild rotation is observed and the stake marks cannot be
confirmed as forward of the blade, please contact your GE representative for further recommendations.

3. Verify staking measurements and radial gaps, during planned bellmouth removal.
During the major inspection or when the bellmouth is removed, first ensure that if there are RO biscuits in place,
both stake marks are forward of or in contact with the blade dovetail. Next, measure each R0 blade's stake
mark geometry and radial gap. The stake height, h, must be greater than 0.030in [0.762mm], and the radial gap
between dovetail bottom and slot bottom/top of biscuit, r, should be les than 0.005in [0.127mm] (see Figure 6).
Due to the difficulty of measuring the stake height, a measurement form is available to calculate the height
based on the measurable dimensions. Any stake marks that do not meet the minimum height should be re-
staked. One exception to this stake height requirement is if the RO blade is exactly flush against the face of the
forward stub shaft with RO biscuits in place. For this scenario, the RO biscuits could have a stake height slightly
less than 0.030in [0.762mm] due to geometrical considerations. If this is observed or the radial gap is
determined to be above limits, it is recommended to contact your GE representative for final engineering
disposition.
