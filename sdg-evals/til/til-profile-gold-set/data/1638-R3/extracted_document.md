TECHNICAL INFORMATION LETTER
F-CLASS R0 AND R1 PLATFORM ULTRASONIC TESTING
APPLICATION
This TIL is applicable to select 6F, 7F, and 9F gas turbines with an 18-stage compressor operating with rotor Stage 0
(R0) and Stage 1 (R1) blades that do not have the platform undercut feature.
PURPOSE
To inform affected users about the recommendation to perform in-situ ultrasonic inspection of the R0 and R1 blade
platforms for evidence of dovetail distress.
REASON FOR REVISION
To communicate about updated recommendations specific to 7F and 9F R1 platform ultrasonic inspection interval
and to update TIL language for clarity.

BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
On GE F-class units with an 18-stage compressor, the row starts with rotor Stage 0 (R0) and ends with stator Stage
17 (S17). Figure 1 shows a cross-section of the F-class compressor front-end.

The compressor rotor blades are positioned and retained by mating the blade bases with dovetail-shaped axial slots
machined on the rotor-wheels. During unit operation, the centrifugal loading of the blades is transmitted through the
planar faces on either side of the blade base to the wheel slot. Once the blade is assembled on to the rotor-wheel,
the loading interface on both the rotor blade and the wheel slot are hidden from view. The surface of the rotor blade
base exposed to the flow-path is referred to as the blade platform. Figure 2 shows a typical cross-section of a rotor
blade assembled in an axial dovetail wheel slot.

In 2005, a 9F unit experienced a compressor event due to R0 distress originating from the suction side of the
dovetail. Subsequent to this, dovetail distress has been identified on R0 and R1 blades within the 7F and 9F fleet. On
R0 blades, the dovetail distress has been noted on both the suction side and pressure side. On R1 blades, the
distress is limited to the suction side.
Based on fleet data collected and investigations performed, the dovetail distress has been attributed to fretting at the
edge of contact region. Figure 3 shows the edge of contact region, where distress occurs. Fretting causes a local
increase in stress at the region, which can lead to crack initiation and propagation with subsequent operation. Once
the cracks grow to a critical depth, there is increased potential for a blade liberation.

A visual inspection or penetrant inspection may not be suitable to detect the dovetail distress that may cause the
previously mentioned compressor events. Hence, in-situ ultrasonic testing of the R0 and R1 platform is
recommended to detect any dovetail distress and help reduce the risk of a blade liberation.
RECOMMENDATIONS
GE recommends performing in-situ dovetail ultrasonic testing (DT-UT) of R0 and R1 blades on a recurrent basis to
help mitigate the risk of blade liberation due to dovetail distress. The recommended inspections are as listed below:
R0: DT-UT of suction side and pressure side.
R1: DT-UT of suction side.
This recommendation only applies to the standard or non-enhanced R0 (including the P-cut R0) and R1 blades
without the platform undercut feature.
7F and 9F, R0 and R1 Inspections
For 7F and 9F units, it is recommended that the R0 and R1 DT-UT be performed the sooner of 8,000 actual fired
hours (FH) or 150 actual fired starts (FS). For peaking units performing more than 150 actual fired starts in a year, it
is recommended to conduct the DT-UT just before and after the peak season.
It may be beneficial to incorporate the DT-UT during the annual borescope inspection, or during forward compressor
inspections per TIL 1509 and TIL 1603, when access and expertise are available. GE has the capability to perform
the R0 and R1 DT-UT without casing removal.
6F R0 and R1 Inspections
For 6F units, it is recommended the R0 and R1 DT-UT be performed during the Major Inspection (MI), or at any
opportunity when the upper-half compressor casing is removed.
Recommendations for units with Enchanced Compressor Packages Installed
For units that have installed Enhanced Compressor Packages (ECP) 2, 2+, 3 or 4, only the R1 DT-UT is necessary
since the enhanced R0 blades have the platform undercut feature. For units that have installed ECP 5, this TIL is not
applicable since both the enhanced R0 and R1 blades included in ECP 5 have the platform undercut feature.
Consult your GE Gas Power Service representative for additional information or for assistance in identifying the type
of R0 or R1 installed on the unit.

Planning for the in-situ DT-UT
The in-situ R0 DT-UT can typically be carried out in 12 to 16 hours following applicable cool down and inlet entry
requirements. R1 DT-UT can typically be carried out within 12 hours following applicable cool down and inlet entry
requirements. The in-situ DT-UT may be incorporated with standard compressor front-end inspections such as TIL
1509 and TIL 1603. Please note that this ultrasonic testing requires rotor rotation.
Ultrasonic Test Results
Once the DT-UT has been completed on all 32 R0 and 32 R1 blades, and the test technician or the GE Gas Power
Services representative confirms that no indications requiring further evaluation have been identified, the unit is
cleared for continued service, pending completion of other parallel outage activities.
In the event of a positive indication during the DT-UT, please contact your local GE Gas Power Services
representative to evaluate the DT-UT results. If the indications are identified as dovetail cracks, it may be
recommended to replace the entire set of blades (if R0), even if the indications are limited to fewer blades. If R1
indications are confirmed, the rotor would require to be sent to a GE Service Center to replace the full set of R1
blades. Replacement of the set would be to account for R0 and R1 dovetail distress that may exist on the set of
blades but falls below DT-UT detectable limits.
Failure to comply with these recommendations may result in blade liberation and potentially result in downstream
compressor distress.
This TIL can be considered complete when the R0 and R1 blades are replaced with blades with platform undercut
feature.
