BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
7FA.02, 7FA.03, 7FA.04, 9FA.02, 9FA.03, and 9FA.04 heavy-duty gas turbines have compressor wheels which carry
blades inserted into dovetail slots broached axially into the wheel rims. These slots can have flat bottoms that
correspond to the dovetail design on the compressor rotor blades. The blades are secured in place via staking of the
forward and aft wheel rim faces.
During start-up, the outer diameters of the aft compressor wheels (the rims) heat up quicker than the internal portions
of the wheels creating thermal gradients inside the wheels. During shutdown, the opposite occurs - the wheel rims cool
off quicker than the internal portions of the wheels. This results in compressive stress at the wheel rims during start-up
and tensile stress on the wheel rims at shutdown.
Figure 1: Thermal Gradients at Start-Up (left) and Shutdown (right) for an F-Class Compressor Rotor
During these thermal transients, at start-up and shutdown, stresses are elevated at the acute corners of the forward
and aft ends of the dovetail slots. The acute angle is defined by the axial centerline of the slot and the plane of the rim
face. After a number of start-up/shutdown cycles, cracks can begin to appear in the dovetail acute corners, and may
propagate in two directions - radially inward along the forward and aft wheel rim faces and forward and aft along the
length of the dovetail slots. Crack initiation and propagation is dependent on several factors such as specific
component geometry, material properties, and operational considerations. Dovetail indications can initiate after a few
hundred start-up/shutdown cycles and may be measurable during inspection after approximately 1,700 to 2,200 starts
on the rotor.

Once cracks initiate, their axial and radial growth is contingent primarily on the frequency and method of start-
up/shutdown cycles. Crack growth axially within the dovetail slot can eventually weaken the post between the dovetail
slots, making the compressor susceptible to blade liberation. Additionally, blade retention characteristics may change
sufficiently to cause a shift in blade frequency responses, which could lead to High Cycle Fatigue (HCF) effects such as
fretting wear on the wheel, blade cracking, and possible blade liberation. Crack growth radially on the wheel rim face
could potentially reach the inner diameter of the rim face, turn under the rim, and begin propagating into the wheel
web. In this scenario, the structural integrity of the wheel could be affected and potentially lead to a wheel burst that
may not be contained by the casing.

RECOMMENDATIONS
Inspections
In order to identify and measure any existing indications, affected rotors accumulating between 1,300 and 2,200 actual
fired starts on one or more of the Stage 12-17 compressor wheels must be inspected per below
Units without OpFlex Fast Ramp installed - Inspect between 1,700 and 2,200 actual fired starts
Units with OpFlex Fast Ramp installed - Inspect between 1,300 and 1,800 actual fired starts
GE has developed, and is continuing to develop, multiple inspection techniques for various accessibility conditions of the
rotor. The particular inspection technique(s) used for a given rotor inspection may vary depending on equipment and
personnel availability. As new inspection techniques and processes are developed or refined, they will be made
available once GE field personnel are trained.
These inspections should be performed by GE's Life Extension Services (LES) group. Working closely with Product Service
and the rotor design engineers, LES has developed inspection techniques and data recording methods that yield the
most accurate inspection results, and allow for analysis and disposition as rapidly as possible. While inspections
performed by third parties and even other GE field service groups may yield some qualitative information, such
inspections will not contain sufficient quantitative accuracy to allow for thorough analysis and disposition.
If the rotor can only be inspected with the upper half of the Compressor Discharge Casing (CDC) installed, then a
borescope inspection (BI) will be performed of the aft rim face of the Stage 17 wheel. (It may be possible to also inspect
the forward rim face of the Stage 17 wheel and the aft rim face of the Stage 16 wheel using the BI probe.) If the upper
half of the CDC can be removed, then several inspection techniques may be utilized. A digital microscope inspection of
the aft rim face of the Stage 17 wheel can be performed. If inspection via digital microscope is not possible, a
Fluorescent Penetrant Inspection (FPI) may be performed. In addition to the digital microscope inspection or FPI, the
forward rim face of the Stage 17 wheel - and both rim faces of the Stage 13 aft-16 wheels - should be inspected via
ultrasonic testing (UT) when the upper half of the CDC can be removed. (It should be noted that although CW12 and
CW13 forward is no longer required to be inspected in a field inspection, when the CW13 thru CW17 flat slot bottom
wheels are replaced, the CW12 must be changed out concurrently.)
In order to ensure clear visibility of any indications using the digital microscope, a thorough cleaning of the aft rim face
around the dovetail slots and compressor wheel/shaft rims should be completed prior to the inspection. Once clean,
perform an optical inspection using the digital microscope to determine indication measurements. The rotor may be
inspected either in the unit with the upper half casings off with the ability to turn the rotor, or out of the unit on stands.
The particular inspection technique(s) used for a given rotor inspection may vary depending on equipment and
personnel availability. However, any and all inspections performed will be certified as complete and accurate by GE. As
new inspection techniques and processes are developed or refined, GE will make them available once they are certified
as complete and accurate, and field personnel are trained.
The upper limits of 1,800 actual fired starts for units with OpFlex Fast Ramp installed or 2,200 actual fired starts for units

cause a failure. The lower limits of 1,300 actual fired starts for units with OpFlex Fast Ramp installed or 1,700 actual
fired starts for units without OpFlex Fast Ramp is set to allow cracks to initiate and propagate sufficiently to be easily
and accurately measured, and to be statistically significant for analysis and disposition purposes. If one or more of the

Repairs
GE has developed repairs for 7FA.02, 7FA.03, 7FA.04, 9FA.02, 9FA.03, 9FA.04 Stage 12-17 FSB compressor wheels that
remove indications. The maximum indication size that can be removed depends on the component, and the feasibility
of the repair will depend upon the rotor condition (e.g. - disassembled in a GE service center, at site on rotor stands, or
in the unit).
In cases where the rotor will not be disassembled and the only option for monitoring indication growth on the Stage 17
shaft over an extended period is via borescope inspection, GE recommends to not repair Stage 17 shaft indications
even if they are within repair limits. If such indications are repaired, the ability to correlate the Stage 17 shaft indication
propagation with indications on other wheels will be lost. If the Stage 17 shaft indications are repaired, the only option
for annually inspecting indications on other wheels would likely require the rotor to be exposed.
Rebuild
Beginning in 2001, GE began introducing Round Slot Bottom (RSB) compressor wheels into new F-class compressor
rotors. The RSB wheels, applicable for Stages 12-17, reduce the stress concentrations at the dovetail slot corners
thereby lowering the stresses during start-up and shutdown transients. In order to permanently remove a rotor from
applicability of this TIL, replacement of the existing FSB wheels with RSB wheels would be necessary. RSB dovetails
significantly lower the stress in the dovetail acute corners, resulting in longer life of these components.

Operational
Actions can be taken to delay the initiation and slow the propagation of FSB dovetail indications:
Minimize start-up and shutdown to the maximum extent possible
For sites with multiple FSB units, prioritize start-ups and shutdowns to units with lower start rotors
Utilize turndown when possible, in lieu of shutting down. However, load swings in a decreasing direction do
induce tensile stresses on the flat slot bottom dovetail indications and therefore need to be accounted for once
indications have been detected on a rotor to properly perform re-inspection intervals and maintain safe
operation. The discussed rotor load swing factoring is only required once indications/cracks have been
observed in an inspection. The factoring is required to be applied to all dispositioned re-inspection intervals as
noted below in section titled Load Swing Details and Factors.
Minimize forced cool-downs upon shutdown as much as possible. If a forced cool down is required it is strongly
recommended to shutdown the unit following a normal shutdown sequence and cool down at turning gear
speed for 8 hours, at which time, then the unit can be placed into forced cool (or crank cool) to accelerate the
remainder of the cool down cycle without peak stresses occurring above the levels of a normal shutdown
cycle.
Perform a Full Speed No Load (FSNL) hold upon shutdown when feasible (at least 30 minutes, up to 60 minutes)
Perform warm restarts (within 8-10 hours of shutdown) to the maximum extent possible
Minimize Fast Starts as much as possible
Please note that certain modifications to the operation of the gas turbine may affect the initiation and propagation of
FSB dovetail cracks. For example, certain features of OpFlex Fast Start - specifically, Fast Acceleration and Fast Load -
are not recommended on units with FSB Stage 12-17 compressor wheels. Please contact your GE Power
representatives to discuss any installed or planned modifications that may fall into the category described in this
paragraph.
Load Swing Details and Factors
Table 1: Decreasing Load Swing Factors
These load swing factors are only required to be applied to load swings that involve a load decrease (load
increasing movements are not required to be factored and may be at normal load movement rates without
any factor to apply).
These load swing factors apply for all load swing rates above 1 MW/min. Decreasing load movements
completed at 1 MW/min or less have no applied factor regardless of the load swing amount. (As noted in
previous section, no factoring and no rate connection is applied for load increasing movement.)
The load swing amount in the table is based on % of full load of the given specific unit involved.
