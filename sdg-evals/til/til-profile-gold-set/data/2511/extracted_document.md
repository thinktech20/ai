TECHNICAL INFORMATION LETTER
F.05 & HA COMPRESSOR-SECTION RETENTION-KEY IMPROVEMENTS
APPLICATION
This TIL is applicable to all 7F.04-200, 7F.05, 7HA.01, 7HA.02, 7HA.03, 9F.05-18, 9HA.01, and 9HA.02 gas turbines.
PURPOSE
To inform affected users about:
1. The potential for keys retaining sub-assemblies such as stator vane segments, inducer, high pressure packing seal, aft-
inducer seal, and/or casing treatment ring to overcome staked retention, which can result in object drops during casing
lifts.
2. The availability of an improved key retention method, and the recommendation to install the improved key retention
modification during a planned major inspection.

BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
Retention keys are used in the upper-half (UH) compressor-section casings to retain stator vane segments, seals - such as high-
pressure packing seal (HPPS), and aft inducer seal (AIS)1, inducer², and casing treatment (CT) ring³ during unit assembly. The
retention keys are installed into a key slot machined on the casing horizontal joint (HJ) on both the left-hand-side (LHS) and right-
hand-side (RHS), and the casing is staked at multiple places around the key slot to displace material over the key, thereby
locking it in place. Figure 1 shows compressor stator keys retained by staking, and Figure 2 shows HPPS key retained by
staking.
Figure 1: Compressor stator keys retained by staking
Figure 2: HPPS key retained by staking
GE Vernova has used staking for retention of several gas turbine (GT) stationary parts, including stator vane, inducer, and seal
keys. While the experience with staked retention method has proven effective, there were two reported incidents where stator
vane retention keys fell out during UH casing lifts. These incidents are described in PSSB 20210505A.

In both instances, follow-up inspections of the key slot showed the stakes had not displaced adequate material over the key to
retain it, which allowed the key and the vane segments to fall out as the casing was being lifted. Figure 3 shows the condition of
the stakes on one of the affected units. There were no injuries from either incident. The affected components in both cases were
blended, and new stakes were placed to adequately retain the key prior to returning the unit to service.
Figure 3: Staking condition on the affected unit
If the retention stakes are not adequately applied, there is potential for keys and the sub-assemblies, such as stator
vane segments, inducer, and seals to drop during UH casing lifts, which can lead to personnel injury.
RECOMMENDATIONS
GE Vernova has engineered an alternate method to help improve key retention. The improvement consists of an upgraded key
that is primarily retained by flanged screws, and the key is additionally staked, which serves as secondary retention. The
modification requires one-time machining of threaded holes on the casing horizontal joint to accept the retention screws. Figure 4
shows details of the improved key retention used on the stator-vane segments, and Figure 5 shows the improved key retention
method used on HPPS, AIS, and the inducer.

It is recommended that the newly configured retention key modification be applied to the unit at the next major inspection (MI)
when all the compressor section upper-half (UH) casings will be removed. The modification may optionally be applied at an
earlier opportunity, if all the affected compressor section UH casings are removed prior to an MI. Once the modification is
completed, any subsequent key/sub-assembly removal and/or installation does not require any machining tools or special tooling.
Contact your regional GE Vernova representative for additional information or assistance with implementing this improved key
retention modification.
Note: Prior to lifting any UH casing, GE Vernova recommends the site personnel to take additional precautions and, if possible, to
inspect the retention keys for any potential abnormalities such as a displaced key. Once the UH casing is separated, telescoping
mirrors may be used to perform this inspection.
The TIL can be considered as complete once the newly configured key retention modification is applied to retain the stator-vanes,
HPPS, AIS1, inducer², and CT ring³.
Note that AIS1, inducer², and CT ring³ are installed only on specific gas turbine models. Contact your GE Vernova representative
to determine the specific components that are recommended to be modified on your gas turbine model.
1 AIS is installed only on HA.0x units.
2 Inducer is installed only on HA.Ox units with a double-wall compressor discharge casing.
3 CT ring is installed only on 7HA.03 units.
SCOPE OF WORK
1. Machine threaded holes and counterbores on the UH compressor-section casings at the HJ to accept retention screws.
2. Install new keys to retain stator-vanes, HPPS, AIS1, inducer², and CT ring³; torque retention screws to specification.
3. Stake around the keys at 4x places to serve as secondary retention.
TIL COMPLETION CRITERIA
The TIL is considered as complete once the newly configured key retention modification is installed.
