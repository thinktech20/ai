BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
A gas turbine rotor is an assembly of shafts, wheels, disks, and spacers. A means of balance is incorporated into each of
these rotor components and the various assemblies. A balance weight groove (BWG) is one example of a feature that is
used to provide a means of balancing. The BWG is an annular channel that is machined into a rotor component that provides
a location for weights to be installed for balance purposes on either the piece part or as a rotor assembly. This annular
channel may contain two BWG entry slots, as shown in Figure 1, located approximately 180 degrees apart that allow for the
balance weights to be easily installed and located in the appropriate locations on the wheel or shaft.
Figure 1: BWG Entry Slots - Correct Machining
During non-destructive evaluation (NDE) of a distance piece (DP) as part of a rotor life extension (RLE), a crack-like
indication was observed in the corner of a balance weight groove entry slot. Both grooves on the specific part showed similar
indications as depicted in Figure 2. GE has inspected additional parts with similar features and observed similar indication
findings.
Figure 2: Indications Found on DP During RLE - Observed Machining Issue
Upon completion of a root cause investigation, GE determined the cause is associated with the variation in the corner
condition of the balance weight groove entry slots and interactions with the various stresses during gas turbine start-up,
shutdown, and loaded operation (gravity sag, etc). Depending upon the specific nature of a units operation and the specific
condition of the part slot corner, crack-like indications may form over the lifetime of the component. If these crack-like
indications propagate to a large enough size, it may be possible for continued rapid crack growth to occur associated with
gravity sag related bending of the rotor as it rotates (high cycle fatigue related mechanism). If not mitigated, the fracture
propagation may lead the CAS and/or DP to separate into two separate pieces. Separation of either shaft into two pieces
may create the possibility for components to exit the gas turbine leading to the possibility for INJURY TO
PERSONNEL/INDIVIDUALS and substantial damage to adjacent equipment.

RECOMMENDATIONS
It is recommended to perform fluorescent penetrant inspection (FPI) for all BWG entry slots on the CAS and DP during the
next planned outage for the unit Hot Gas Path (HGP) or Major Inspection (MI) at or before the rotor reaches a total of
378,000 equivalent cold rotor turning time.
For case where FPI is clear of any indications - evaluate all the balance entry slot corner conditions with replication process.
If any balance weight entry slot corner condition presents with a <0.060" fillet size - perform entry slot blending, FPI,
and peening operations to mitigate the risk of crack initiation and propagation.
Corner specific item - if any portion of a specific balance weight groove corner meets noted criteria, then
perform modification machining on the entire balance weight groove entry slot corner.
Fillet > 0.060" and uniformly this size over full length of the BWG entry slot corner - leave in as-found condition
For case with observable FPI indication/indications - contact GE engineering through an ER case for technical disposition.

Equivalent cold rotor turning time is defined as a spinning rotor in combination with cool metal temperatures (generally
speaking conditions with all wheelspace temperatures measuring less than 150 degrees F). Turning gear (TG) operation,
forced cooling operation, offline water wash operation, and unit startups are the periods where cool rotor conditions exist and
contribute to the overall equivalent cold rotor turning time. Equivalent refers to a standardization of the # of high cycle fatigue
cycles for a given hour and therefore has a component associated with the accumulation of cycles relative to the rotor speed.
For example, at purge speed (~20% speed) versus turning gear speed (~6 rpm) a higher # of stress cycles will occur per
minute of operation due solely to the increased rotational speed of the rotor for a given period.
