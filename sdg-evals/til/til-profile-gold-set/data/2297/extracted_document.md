TECHNICAL INFORMATION LETTER
FLARED 7F & 9F PRE-ENHANCED COMPRESSOR R1 BLADE RECOMMENDATIONS
APPLICATION
This TIL is applicable to flared 7F and 9F gas turbines operating with pre-enhanced (standard) rotor stage-1 (R1) blades
that meet any of the following criteria:
Units that have had prior R1 tip rubs, or tip indications, or tip corner loss, or tip repairs.
Units that are currently operating with or have previously operated with uniform spaced S0 and S1 vanes.
Units that show visible signs of corrosion pitting in the forward compressor.
PURPOSE
To purpose of this TIL is to inform affected users of the risk of R1 tip cracks, or tip corner liberations and to provide
recommendations to help improve unit availability and reliability.

BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
During a planned compressor borescope inspection on a flared 7F gas turbine in mid-2020, two (2) pre-enhanced R1
blades were identified with tip corner loss. An additional (1) R1 blade was identified with a tip crack. Figure 1 shows the
location of the R1 blade within the F-class compressor, and Figure 2 shows one of the R1 blades that was noted with a
liberated tip corner. The liberated R1 tip corners resulted in considerable distress to the downstream compressor section.
Owing to the extent of distress, the compressor rotor required replacement. Multiple stator vanes were also replaced as a
part of unit rebuild activities.

Laboratory evaluation of the three (3) affected R1 blades following the incident revealed that the cracks initiated from
corrosion pits located near the blade tip. While a visual inspection of the affected R1 blade tips did not show any
evidence of rubs, material analysis under magnification identified a heat affected zone, up to .100" deep, at the blade tip;
see Figure 3. The heat affected zone was noted with reduced hardness and over-aged microstructure. The rotor had
also previously operated with uniform spaced S0 and S1 vanes. The investigation concluded that the R1 tip cracking
initiated due to the combined effect of degraded material properties at the blade tip from prior rubs and corrosion pitting,
and previous operation with uniform spaced S0 and S1 vanes.

The above finding is consistent with the R1 tip distress mechanism described in TIL 1509. While the R1 blades have a
substantial operation margin, corrosion pitting and heavy rubs can severely degrade the material's fatigue capability.
Field data suggests that flared 7F and 9F units with pre-enhanced R1 blades with prior tip rubs, and current or previous
operation with uniform spaced S0 and S1 vanes are at higher risk of experiencing R1 tip cracks.
Note: Most R1 tip corner loss events result in only minor downstream distress that can be blended in place. R1 blend
repair requires lifting the upper-half compressor casing.
RECOMMENDATIONS
GE recommends the following actions be performed to help mitigate the risk of R1 tip rubs and R1 tip events:
Follow the operational and maintenance best practices to avoid or limit the likelihood of heavy tip rubs as
described in TIL 1509.
During planned or annual borescope inspections, monitor the R1 blade tips for indications of rubs and evidence
of corrosion pitting.
If R1 tip rubs are identified, or if R1 tips had previously rubbed or repaired, or if the rotor has previously
operated with or currently operating with uniform spaced S0 and S1 vanes, perform a non-destructive inspection
(NDI) of all R1 blades as described in TIL 1509.
If new R1 tip rubs or indications are identified, or if R1 tip indications are repaired, perform repeat R1 tip NDI
per the schedule described in TIL 1509.
Note: A light or moderate R1 tip rub may not manifest into an indication in the short term and it could take several start-
stop cycles following the rub event before an indication is identified. Conversely, a heavy tip rub may manifest into
multiple, repeat indications, even if the tips have been previously polished, or blend repaired. It is therefore imperative
that R1 tip NDI be performed per TIL 1509 recommendations if there have been any prior rubs.

In addition to the above outlined operation, maintenance, and inspection best practices, in order to increase unit reliability
and availability, consider installing a full set of enhanced R1 blades whenever the unit rotor is sent to a GE repair shop
and scope requires unstacking the compressor rotor. Optionally, consider unstacking the compressor rotor to replace the
R1 blades at the next opportunity the unit rotor is sent to a GE repair shop.
Enhanced R1 blades are full airfoil shot-peened and have a higher damage tolerance when compared to the pre-
enhanced R1 blades. Further, installing enhanced R1 blades would improve unit availability as TIL 1509 inspection scope
is limited to an annual BI, and TIL 1638 inspection scope to perform R1 dovetail ultrasonic testing is eliminated.
This TIL can be considered complete when the above recommendations are incorporated into the site's maintenance
planning practices, or when the full set of R1 blades are replaced. Until the full set of R1 blades are replaced, it is
recommended to continue with the operation, maintenance, and inspection guidelines per this TIL and TIL 1509.
