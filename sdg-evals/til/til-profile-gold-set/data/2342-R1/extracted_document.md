BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
The exhaust frame assembly consists of the structural part and the diffuser. The cavity between the structural part and
diffuser is cooled by externally supplied air. To assure that the components can move independently due to thermal
difference, flex seals are introduced on the outer barrel forward side as shown in Figure 1.
Structural Part
Exhaust Diffuser
Figure 1: Flex seal location
Historically, flex seals have in some cases experienced wear, cracking, misalignment, bending, disengagement, and/or
liberation.
RECOMMENDATIONS
GE recommends inspecting the units in operation that have installed the GEN0, GEN1, or GEN2 flex seal configuration
during yearly planned borescope inspections. Based on fleet experience, if the flex seals are inspected through the hot gas
path section, limited areas of the lower section can be captured.
If any of the following indications are observed during planned borescope inspections, report findings to engineering for
disposition:
Signs of wear at the exhaust frame pipe/ring or flange side
Cracks, misalignment, bending, disengagement, and/or liberation
It is also recommended to monitor the unit for 3AO elevated wheel space temperatures and exhaust frame blower alarms;
report any unusual observations to engineering for disposition.
Refer to Figure 2 for representative pictures of different flex seal configurations.
This TIL is applicable as long as GEN0, GEN1, or GEN2 flex seal configuration is installed. Please contact your GE
representative if you have any additional questions.
