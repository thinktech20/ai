F-CLASS FRONT END (R0, S0, AND R1) COMPRESSOR INSPECTIONS
APPLICATION
This TIL is applicable to all 6F, 7F, and 9F gas turbines with an 18-stage axial compressor.
PURPOSE
The purpose of this TIL is to provide compressor front-end (R0, S0, R1) inspection recommendations to help improve
unit availability and reliability.
REASON FOR REVISION
To update the criteria that warrants R1 blade tip non-destructive inspections, to recommend performing the described
non-destructive inspections during major inspections, and to update TIL language for improved clarity.

BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
On GE F-class units with an 18-stage compressor, the row starts with rotor stage-0 (R0) and ends with stator stage-17
(S17). Figure 1 shows a cross-section of the F-class compressor front-end.

Some F-class gas turbines have experienced front end compressor distress in the past. The noted distress falls into
three distinct categories as listed below.
1. R0 leading edge (LE) root distress.
2. R0 and R1 blade tip distress.
3. S0 trailing edge (TE) distress.
Note: Field data suggests the 6F compressor is at lower risk for front end compressor distress.
1. R0 LE Root Distress:
R0 LE root distress has been attributed to the following:
Object damage, either foreign or other, leading to stress concentration.
LE erosion from water ingestion that is not inspected or maintained per recommendations described in
the latest revisions of TILs 1323, 1399, 1603, 1518 and operation and maintenance (O&M) manuals. This
TIL does not modify those recommendations.
Corrosion pitting due to ingestion of corrosive elements through the inlet.
Once a crack has initiated, it propagates during the start and stop cycles of normal operation. Subsequently, the crack
can propagate at full speed operation due to high cycle fatigue and potentially lead to blade liberation. The liberation of
an R0 blade from the root region can cause collateral damage to the gas turbine. Figure 2 shows examples of R0 LE
root distress.

2. R0 and R1 Blade Tip Distress:
R0 and R1 blade tip distress has been attributed to the following:
Tip rubs.
Corrosion pitting.
During unit operation, the blades and rotor wheels elongate due to centrifugal forces and due to thermal expansion.
The blade tips can rub against the casings if the thermal growth of the casing does not match with the radial growth of
the rotor. The rubs are typically light but can be heavy in some conditions. Heavy tip rubs result in rolled-over metal
burrs with potential material properties degradation. In this condition, tip cracks could initiate during start-up and shut-
down cycles. Subsequent starts could propagate this crack and may eventually result in a tip corner loss. A tip corner
loss may result in a forced outage to repair the affected downstream blades. Figure 3 shows examples of tip rubs with
rolled over metal and tip crack.
Figure 3 - Examples of blade tip rubs with rolled over metal and tip crack
Although it is expected that some rubs will occur in the normal course of operation, it is imperative to limit the number
and magnitude of heavy tip rubs. The following factors can aggravate tip rubs.
Overspeed trip tests: Overspeed trip tests can cause casing distortion if not performed per TIL 1345.
Improper start-up procedures for faster start-up times: Starting the unit outside the provided control
procedure may lead to inadequate blade clearances. Refer to TIL 1348 for select 7F units.

Opening of turbine compartment doors during unit operation: The turbine compartment ventilation system
regulates the casings' temperature during all aspects of unit operation. Opening the compartment doors during
unit operation disturbs the natural convection currents, which may distort the casings.
Fluid spills on casings: Fluid spills on casings during operation can cause distortion. It is recommended that
routine maintenance of the piping and tubing within the turbine enclosure is performed as detailed in TIL 1579.
Foundation distress: Improper construction of the turbine foundation or uneven settling of the earth may lead
to turbine foundation cracks or displacement. This can lead to mechanical distortion of the gas turbine casings
and misalignment of the rotor. It is recommended that routine inspections of the foundation for cracking and gas
turbine support key clearances be conducted. Contact your GE Gas Power Services representative for
assistance.
Wet operation: Visible water droplets from poorly maintained evaporative coolers or inlet foggers, or
rainwater from poorly maintained inlet filtration systems may enter the unit and can collect and coalesce on inlet
surfaces causing local thermal distortion of the inlet casing and compressor casing. These systems must be
inspected and maintained per the O&M manual recommendations.
3. S0 TE Distress:
S0 TE distress has been attributed to damping loss due to vane lock-up. The distress location varies from the vane
root to approximately 50% span. Multiple cracks may be observed on a single vane, and several vanes may be
cracked in one unit.
The S0 vanes installed at the bottom-dead-centre are more likely to experience distress, however, the distress is not
limited to this location. The cracks are driven by starts. Figure 4 shows examples of S0 TE distress.
Figure 4 - Examples of SO TE distress
RECOMMENDATIONS
The following maintenance program is recommended to reduce the risk of front-end compressor distress. It includes
four types of inspections:
Visual inspection of the R0 LE root, R0 and R1 tip, and S0 TE.
Non-destructive inspection (NDI) of the R0 LE root.
NDI of the R0 and R1 tip.
NDI of the S0 TE.

1. Visual Inspections:
An annual visual inspection of the compressor front end is recommended as part of the annual borescope inspection.
Visually inspect the R0 LE root region for cracks, nicks, dents, erosion or corrosion pitting. The LE root
region extends 1" above the platform, and 2" downstream from the LE on both the pressure and suction side.
Visually inspect the R0 and R1 blades for missing tips, corrosion pits, visible cracks, rolled over material or
burrs, and other evidence of rubs.
Visually inspect the S0 TE for cracks. Cracks may initiate between the root and approximately 50% span.
Note: Care should be taken to prevent foreign material inclusion into the inlet plenum during inlet entry and closure.
During the visual inspection:
If R0 LE root cracks are observed, perform an NDI of all R0 root locations immediately. If R0 LE root cracks
exist and are confirmed by NDI, do not restart the unit.
If R0 or R1 tip corner loss is observed, perform an NDI of all R0 and R1 tips immediately. If R0 or R1
indications exist and are confirmed by NDI, do not restart the unit. Additionally, perform a borescope inspection
of the entire compressor at the first opportunity.
If S0 TE cracks are observed, perform an NDI of all S0 TE immediately. If S0 trailing edge cracks exist and
are confirmed by NDI, do not restart the machine.
NDI of the R0 root, R0 and R1 tip, and S0 TE may be performed in lieu of a visual inspection.

3. NDI on standard (non enhanced) R0 and R1 blade tip:
The below recommendations are for standard (non-enhanced) R0 and R1 blades only. If the unit has received
enhanced compressor package 2, or higher, then R0 tip NDI is not required. If the unit has received enhanced
compressor package 5, then R1 tip NDI is not required.
If no rub indications are present on R0 or R1 blade tips on units with more than 100 total actual fired starts,
continue to monitor with visual inspections.
On units with fewer than 100 actual fired starts perform visual or borescope inspection on R0 and R1 blade
tips at the next planned outage after accumulating 100 actual fired starts.
If rubs are identified, perform an NDI of the R0 and R1 tips during first discovery and then again after 25, 50,
and 100 actual fired starts to verify that tip cracks have not formed.
If rolled-over metal, burrs, cracks, or tip loss is noted, repairs should be completed at the first opportunity.
Until repairs are completed, an NDI of the R0 and R1 tips should be performed every 12 actual fired starts to
avoid tip corner loss. Tip grinding may be required to improve blade tip clearances to help prevent future rubs.
Following the repair, a repeat NDI of the R0 and R1 tips is recommended at 25, 50, and 100 actual fired starts
from the time of the repair to verify that tip cracks have not formed.
Once the initial inspections and, or repairs are complete, continue to perform NDI of the R0 and R1 blade
tips per schedule described in Table 1 for 7F and 9F, and Table 2 for 6F.
If the unit is suspected to be in a corrosive environment and the unit is not equipped with non-uniform vane
spacing (NUVS) S0 and S1 vanes, it is recommended to perform an NDI on the R0 and R1 blade tips every 40
to 50 starts. Also inspect for corrosion pits and any other linear indications on the R0 and R1 blades.
If R0 or R1 tip indications exist and are confirmed by NDI, contact your GE Gas Power Services representative for
evaluation.
7F and 9F R0 blends or repairs may be performed in situ through the inlet. 7F and 9F R1 blends or repairs requires
lifting the upper-half compressor casing. 6F R0 and R1 repairs requires lifting the upper half mid compressor casing.

5. Corrosive environment concerns (all F-class compressors):
Corrosion poses two risks to the compressor. Firstly, corrosion pitting can lead to airfoil distress. Additionally, corrosion
build up at the stator ring segment may cause the vane to lock up in the ring, leading to damping loss. Evidence of a
corrosive environment would include but is not limited to:
Significantly damaged, degraded, or continually wet filter media.
Signs of corrosion on inlet and upstream components or structure.
Visible signs of any deposits or residue in the inlet or on the filter media.
Fleet experience has shown that cracks can initiate from corrosion pits present on airfoils. The potential for cracking is
dependent on size, severity and location of pitting on the airfoils. If pitting or cracks are observed on any airfoils,
please submit inspection results to your GE Gas Power Services representative for evaluation.
