BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
The compressor section of the F-Class gas turbines has 18 (0-17) stages of rotating components (rotor blades) and stationary
components (stator vanes).
During operation, certain conditions such as low ambient temperature and part load can cause flow separation on the S17
vanes due to the inner barrel counterbore cavity. TILs 1315-2R1 and 1478-2 recommend installing counterbore plugs and
modifying the control system to reduce aft end distress.
Welded bolt configuration:
In 2004, a shrouded S17 was introduced to operate without any restrictions to the control logic. The shrouded configuration
includes a contoured tip, which reduces flow separation and enables more uniform airflow. The S17 tenon is fixed to the shroud
using a bolt and bushing configuration, where the bolt is secured via one or two tack welds (Figure 1).

Engineering analysis on the segments introduced in 2008 revealed the causes for the distress as follows:
Relative motion between bushing and shroud caused by flow induced vibration resulting in wear on the bushing, at
surfaces interfacing with shroud.
Bushing material (316 SST) is sacrificial to shroud material.
Bushing wear leads to additional shroud motion and eventual shroud wear.
Wear can accelerate due to low ambient and partial load conditions.
Staked bolt configuration with changed bushing material:
In 2016, a new shrouded Gen-IV S17 configuration with change in bushing material to cobalt-chromium based alloy having an
improved wear couple was introduced.
During annual borescope inspection, a few fleet leader units with Gen-IV configuration of S17 were found with distress (Figure
7). Investigations revealed that the material couple between the shroud and bushing chosen are not exhibiting the expected
results due to the differences in thermal range for the applications. GE conducted root cause analysis on the worn Gen-IV
configuration, wear damage characterized on bushing, shroud, and airfoil tip shroud interface. Extensive wear testing was
completed, and test results show that the self mated wear couple of shroud-bushing is a better option than previous
generations of wear couples.

RECOMMENDATIONS
The following sections outline the S17 inspection applicable for Gen-IV and earlier mechanically-attached shroud configurations
with and without compressor discharge casing (CDC) removal.
Inspection Procedure 1 - When S17s are NOT exposed:
An annual borescope is recommended per GER 3620. A borescope inspection is also recommended during scheduled outages
such as planned Combustion Inspections (CI) or Hot Gas Path Inspections (HGPI), when the CDC is not removed.
During all borescope inspections, it is recommended to inspect 100% of the shrouded S17 for signs of distress. Distress can be
observed by inspecting along the vane towards the bushing area (Figure 2, 6, and 7) or shroud holes (Figure 7) or between
vane tips and the shroud (Figure 3).
Inspection Procedure 2 - When S17s are exposed:
Major Inspection (MI or if the CDC is removed during a Hot Gas Path Inspection (HGPI))
It is recommended to visually inspect for any signs of bushing wear (Figure 8), tenon damage, shroud hole ovalization (Figure
9), and wear between the vane tips and the shroud. In some cases, the bushings may detach. S17s in the lower half should be
either removed or inspected by borescope.

If significant distress, cracks, detached bushings, or ovalized shroud hole are found during Inspection Procedure 1 or 2, the
damage must be documented with pictures and submitted to your GE Services representative for engineering disposition.
If wear or damage is observed during the inspection above, it is recommended to order and install the latest Gen-V S17
configuration as a set for durability improvement. It is acceptable to replace only the damaged segment of S17 with the latest
S17 segment (Gen-V configuration). All configurations of S17 shrouded segments are compatible and therefore individual
segments may be replaced with the newer configuration. GE also offers refurbishment services to modify earlier mechanically-
attached shroud S17 configurations to the latest Gen-V configuration based on approved repair instruction. Please consult your
service representative for details.
