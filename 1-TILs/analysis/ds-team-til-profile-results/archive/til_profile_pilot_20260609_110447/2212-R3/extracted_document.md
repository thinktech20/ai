BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
GE 7F.05 (same compressor as the 7F.04-200) compressors have a 14-stage compressor starting with rotor stage 1 (R1)
and ending with rotor stage 14 (R14). Directly upstream of the R1 blades are the inlet guide vanes (IGV) that serve to
improve the incoming airflow during all modes of operation (start up, shutdown, part load, and base load operation). In
addition, the first three stages of stator vanes on a 7F.05 are variable stator vanes (VSVs), which actuate to control airflow
through the compressor. T-Fairings are removable hardware that form the rotor flow path in the forward compressor
between rotor stages. For affected 7F.05 (and associated 7F.04-200) compressors, there are two stages of T-Fairings (TF).
The 1-2 T-Fairing is between the stage 1 and stage 2 compressor blades, and the 2-3 T-Fairing is between the stage 2 and
stage 3 compressor blades. The locations of VSV1, VSV2, 1-2 T-Fairing, and 2-3 T-Fairing can be seen in Figure 1.

PC = Number of purge cycles without completing a fired start in conjunction with that purge cycle (#)
For example, if a unit operated for 8,200 hours at normal turning gear speed (6 rpm), then performed 2,400 hours at low
speed turning gear speed, while consuming 450 fired starts and performing 100 purge cycles without completing a fired start
in conjunction, then the equivalent turning gear hours (ETG) is
ETG = 8,200 + 40 + 1,800 + 400 = 10,440 hours
GE has observed that units with greater than 5,000 ETG hours have seen distress requiring inspection management to
occur and units with greater than 10,000 ETG hours have observed wear reaching levels of risk that may require imminent
T-fairing replacement to occur. GE recommends all customers to utilize their current and prior operation as well as their best
projections of operation going forward to project out this ETG calculation value for all planned hot gas path and/or major
inspection outages to accurately plan for Low Speed Turning Gear and/or Enhanced T-fairing with springs procurement
purposes.
RECOMMENDATIONS
The following maintenance program is recommended to reduce the risk of TF and/or VSV material liberation.
GE has developed an enhanced TF configuration to significantly reduce wear and tilting during low speed operation. It is
recommended to ensure that these components are acquired early for planned outages and based on inspection result
dispositions from this time going forward. Additionally, GE has developed, for most 7FA.05 configurations, a Low Speed
Turning Gear solution, which can aid in significantly reducing the accumulation of ETG prior to reaching the first major
inspection to wear and tear on the T-fairings and associated rotor dovetail slot surfaces.
For units with original T-fairings (without springs) and without Low Speed Turning Gear installed, the following is
recommended:
Step A). If the unit is projected to reach the unit's first major inspection of the GT where the compressor case will be
removed as part of the planned outage before completing 5,000 ETG hours, perform normal borescope inspections and
install enhanced T-fairings with springs during the unit's first planned major inspection.
Step B). If the unit is projected to exceed 5,000 ETG prior to reaching the unit's first planned major inspection interval, then
install Low Speed Turning Gear at first available opportunity to reduce risk of needing to replace T-fairings prior to first
planned major inspection outage. Additionally, during any planned borescope inspections on this unit performed after
accumulating greater than 5,000 ETG hours, it is recommended to perform a detailed T-fairing inspection as follows:
1. Inspect 100% of the 1-2 T-Fairings and 2-3 T-Fairings for edge distress, circumferential gaps, misalignments,
tilting, and rubs. Borescope measurements are required to be taken.
2. Inspect 100% of the VSV1s and VSV2s for evidence of tip rubs, tip indications, or tip loss.
