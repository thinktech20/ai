TECHNICAL INFORMATION LETTER
RO COMPRESSOR BLADE AXIAL RETENTION (STAKING) ALTERNATIVES
APPLICATION
All commissioned F-class gas turbines
PURPOSE
To inform operators of the limitations of traditional means of axial retention (peening/staking) for compressor stage 0
rotor blades (ROs) resulting from successive R0 change-outs, and to notify users of a recommended wheel modification
to apply when the traditional means of retention is no longer available. This alternative method uses replacement
inserts as a staking medium that has no limitation on the number of times it can be replaced.
REASON FOR REVISION
To update the 7F and 9F casing lifting and removal requirements.

BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
Many F-Class units have undergone multiple R0 compressor blade replacements in the field. Reasons for such field
replacements have included erosion repairs and application of modified configurations. Traditional means of axial
retention, including OEM assembly, involve a pair of peening (staking) marks applied to the face of the Forward Stub
Shaft (FSS). After two successive R0 replacements, typically there is no remaining space available to appropriately stake
the blade in place (Figure 1).
Figure 1:
Forward face of the FSS: R0 dovetail staking marks. No space remaining to stake
In recent instances where traditional staking locations are exhausted, alternative staking locations have been specified
and applied to allow for further R0 change-outs. These situations have involved both staking along the pressure faces
of the dovetail or along the blade platform flow path surface (see Figures 2 and 3). With a recent engineering
development, these methods are no longer a recommended practice.
Figure 2:
RO blade rim staking marks - not preferred

RECOMMENDATIONS
An alternative R0 staking application has been developed that uses replacement staking inserts that are installed in the
wheel slots when R0 removal is warranted (Figure 4). This allows for a multiple number of R0 replacements, since new
inserts are applied with each blade change-out. This modification is usually needed on the third or greater replacement
of the R0 blades.
This development provides for advantages over interim applications due to the flexibility it offers in multiple R0 change-
outs, as well as affecting geometry and stresses only in the traditionally staked location on the wheel. This method can
be implemented either in the field or in a GE service center, and the process and tooling has been developed to be
accurate and repeatable.
Figure 4:
Pocket machined in dovetail to retain replaceable staking inserts - preferred option
GE recommends this modification to any operator planning to change RO blades for the third or greater time, if the
modification has not previously been applied. The modification should be performed in conjunction with the planned RO
change-out, or at a suitable maintenance opportunity preceding this. Depending on the scope of work required and the
specific unit conditions, the application may require all blades in the stage to be removed in order to have access for
the tooling to fit. For 7F and 9F units, the application can be completed with the inlet bellmouth removed or in a jacked
condition. For 6F units, the application requires that the inlet bellmouth be removed from the unit.
Contact your GE Services representative for availability, further details, or to initiate the CM&U process for RO wheel
modification. This TIL is considered complete when the biscuit modification has been installed.

PLANNING INFORMATION
Compliance
Compliance Category: M
Timing Code: 5
Manpower Skills
Machining of FSS R0 dovetail slots (1x to 32x depending on scope) for the staking inserts should be conducted by GE
Service Center personnel qualified specifically for this purpose. Likewise, R0 removal/installation and staking should be
performed by blade technicians from a GE Service Center.
Parts
Contact your local GE Service Representative for assistance.
Special Tooling
Controlled milling machinery is required for accurate and repeatable drilling of the pockets. Depending on the scope
and unit configuration of a particular R0 blade change project, special milling fixtures are required which either use the
two adjacent dovetail slots or the dovetail slot being machined. It is a possibility, that if staking locations are exhausted
from all 32 R0 blades, the need to replace a single R0 blade may warrant removal of all 32 such that the modification
can be conducted on the entire wheel.
A dual-staking tool has been developed to ensure proper staking geometry and placement. The milling machinery and
the staking devices are provided by the GE Service Center technicians performing the job.
Scope of Work
For 6F units: Removal of the UH inlet bellmouth is required
For 7F and 9F units: The UH of the inlet bellmouth must be jacked ~ 18" off the horizontal joint or may be
removed.
Estimated duration is 3-4 shifts.
Two milling technicians from GE certified repair shop per shift.
One NDT technician needed per shift.
One Field Engineer and scaffolding crew (three craft laborers) needed.
Turning gear/rotating fixture operator (dedicated person) needed.
