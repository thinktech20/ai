BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
The rotor forward shaft is the first wheel of the compressor rotor assembly. The forward shaft bolts to the load coupling
and contains the #1 bearing journal. It retains the first rotating stage (RO), which is located between the inlet guide
vanes (IGV) and the first stationary stage (SO).
R0 Blade
SO Vane
Inlet Guide Vane
Forward shaft
Figure 1: Forward compressor cross-section
Recently, during two flared 7FA outages, cracks were found on the pressure face of the wheel dovetail of the forward
shaft. The cracks initiated at the edge of contact between the blade and wheel and propagated perpendicular to the
pressure face. The affected rotors were sent to a service center to remove the forward shaft from the assembly.
Figure 2:
Rotor forward shaft crack examples
A root cause analysis (RCA) was initiated to determine the causes for crack initiation and propagation. One of the
primary drivers is the RO response at full speed for the 1st Axial (1A) and 2ⁿᵈ Flex (2F) modes. This modal response drives
a wheel vibratory response at full speed. A second driver for flared units is extended operation at turndown.
In addition, certain hardware configurations may increase this wheel stress and make a crack more likely to initiate.
This hardware includes:
Operation with a crack increases the risk of R0 migration, R0 and IGV clashing, or blade and wheel post liberation that
could cause secondary damage to the gas turbine compressor.

RECOMMENDATIONS
No inspections are recommended for any 7FA shipped new with non-uniform S0/S1 vane spacing (flared) or an
enhanced compressor package 5.
7FB units are not affected by this TIL.
For all other units, the inspection intervals in the table below apply as follows:
Table 1: Inspection Intervals
It is recommended to perform a dovetail ultrasonic (UT) inspection on any 7FA unit that has operated for any length of
time with all of the below specified hardware:
The ultrasonic inspection should be performed on all 32 wheel dovetail slots on both the pressure and suction side of
the dovetail.
For a unit that has already exceeded its inspection interval, the inspection should occur at the next interval described
above according to that unit's operating profile, either hours based or starts based.
If the compressor remains in the unenhanced configuration or with uniform S0/S1 vane spacing, reinspections are
recommended at the inspection interval specified above, until the rotor maintenance interval is reached per GER 3620.
If a unit is upgraded to non-uniform SO/S1 vanes or an enhanced compressor package (package 2-5 for flared units,
package 3-5 for unflared units), then only one reinspection is necessary at the time of the upgrade. If the upgrade
occurred prior to the release of this TIL, then only one inspection is necessary at the first interval outlined above.
Submit inspection results and the forward shaft serial number (located on the forward flange outside diameter of the
shaft) to your GE Service Representative for review and additional recommendations.
