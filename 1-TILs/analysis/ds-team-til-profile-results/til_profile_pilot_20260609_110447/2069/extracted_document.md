BACKGROUND DISCUSSION
The turbine rotor in a heavy-duty gas turbine performs several functions, including torque transmission to the
compressor and generator for power generation as well as supporting the turbine blades. Additionally, the forward and
aft rim arms of the turbine 1-2 spacer wheel provide the necessary passageways for cooling air to travel to the first and
second stage turbine blades during operation, as well as creating the mating sealing surfaces with the stage 2 nozzle
diaphragm. The turbine 1-2 spacer wheel is produced utilizing a high nickel alloy of a similar nature to the stage 1 and
2 turbine wheels.

During a recent outage following a high vibration trip, it was observed that a section of the forward rim arm of a 9F 1-2
turbine spacer wheel had liberated during full load operation. Upon liberation of the rim section, the metal piece broke
into two pieces. The smaller piece was found lodged into the cooling slot of the turbine wheel 1 and the remaining
larger piece was ejected into the hot gas path of the unit. This ejection of material into the hot gas path resulted in
significant collateral damage to the compressor and turbine sections of the gas turbine.
Figure 2: Observed turbine rotor 1-2 spacer wheel rim section liberation
Upon completion of a detailed Root Cause Analysis (RCA) into this event, it was determined that the cracks initiated from
the internal surface of the turbine 1-2 spacer wheel rim arm and propagated radially and circumferentially outward
toward the external rim surface. The crack propagated until the rim could no longer sustain the centripetal forces
applied during operation, at which time, the resulting section liberated as noted above. Investigation of the fracture
surface displayed evidence of inter-granular crack propagation consistent with hold time fatigue.

Based on this observed turbine 1-2 spacer wheel rim liberation event, the afore mentioned RCA conclusions, and fleet
experience associated with TIL-1937, rotors operating in higher operational temperatures and humid or corrosive
conditions may pose an increased risk of crack initiation and propagation in the rim arms. Therefore, in order to reduce
the risk of a rim arm liberation, GE recommends performing an inspection of the forward and aft rim arms of any
turbine 1-2 spacer wheels having accumulated greater than 80,000 factored fired hours of operation.
RECOMMENDATIONS
In order to reduce the risk of a turbine 1-2 spacer wheel arm section liberation, GE recommends performing an
ultrasonic inspection of the rim region of the turbine 1-2 spacer wheel at the first outage (either hot gas path or major
inspection) once the turbine 1-2 spacer wheel has accumulated 80,000 factored fired hours of operation (approximate
range for inspection is 80,000 to 96,000 factored fired hours).
It is strongly recommended to engage GE's Inspection & Life Extension Services (I&LES) group to accurately perform this
recommended inspection of the turbine 1-2 spacer wheel rim regions identified. Working closely with GE's gas turbine
rotor engineers, I&LES has developed specific ultra-sonic inspection techniques to fully inspect all necessary rim arm
regions of the turbine 1-2 spacer wheel.
Further inspections will be completed as a part of any rotor life extension processes, therefore this TIL is considered
complete once the inspection mentioned in this recommendations section is completed and dispositioned.
For any questions or to discuss available options please contact your GE service representative for assistance.

Scope of Work
Upon removal of the turbine shell casing for the hot gas path or major outage, a thorough cleaning of the rim of the 1-2
turbine spacer wheel is required to remove any oil and/or residual debris from the outer surface. This required cleaning
can be completed in approximately one shift (~8 hours). Ultrasonic inspection of the turbine 1-2 spacer wheel rim region
can be completed in approximately two shifts (~12-16 hours). These durations are estimates only.
