### What the latest FSR v2 design work adds to your GE positioning

The newer FSR v2 work strengthens GE beyond “I built a large RAG pipeline.” It shows
**architectural judgment across the entire retrieval chain**: source-document structure →
metadata → section/ESN attribution → chunking → embeddings → Vector Search → evidence
seen by the LLM.

#### 1. You rejected a tempting shortcut when the evidence showed it was unsafe

A metadata-only correction looked faster, but you discovered that metadata boundaries and
searchable chunks had been produced using different PDF parsing representations. Their
character offsets did not reliably align. Rather than ship a partial fix that could continue to
mis-attribute evidence, you changed the plan so parsing, section detection, chunking, and
embedding shared one coordinate system.

**Interview signal:** Are Right, A Lot; Dive Deep; Insist on the Highest Standards; Have
Backbone and Commit. This is strong material for “Tell me about a time new information made
you change your technical approach.”

#### 2. You separated deterministic engineering from LLM inference

For known document structures—equipment headings, section hierarchy, parent/child context,
ESN inheritance—you favored deterministic rules because they are explainable, repeatable,
and testable. When the document evidence is genuinely ambiguous, the design keeps the
assignment unresolved or uses a targeted LLM-assisted path rather than asking an LLM to
classify every page.

**Interview signal:** senior AI architecture judgment. You are not using an LLM merely because
one is available; you are choosing where probabilistic reasoning creates value and where it
creates unnecessary cost or variability.

#### 3. You moved from document-level metadata to evidence-level attribution

The root problem was not simply “bad metadata.” Multi-equipment reports could contain a Gas
Turbine and Generator with different ESNs. A document-level label could therefore cause a
retrieval miss or send Generator evidence to the model as Turbine evidence. Your redesign
made equipment identity local to the relevant section/region and propagated that identity to
the searchable evidence.

**Interview signal:** end-to-end systems thinking. You can connect a low-level data-lineage
choice directly to the correctness of an AI recommendation.

#### 4. Region-first chunking is a strong architecture trade-off to discuss

The current pattern chunks the document first and then assigns a region using maximum
character overlap. That allows a chunk to straddle a Gas Turbine → Generator boundary and
forces an attribution guess. The proposed redesign flips the sequence: establish trusted
regions first, then sub-chunk inside each region so every chunk inherits one equipment
identity deterministically and cross-region overlap is zero.

**Interview signal:** Invent and Simplify + Highest Standards. This is an elegant example of
removing an entire class of downstream exceptions by changing the boundary of the upstream
abstraction.

#### 5. You designed for provenance instead of silent fallback

The revised design carries fields such as `esn_source`, `esn_confidence`, `region_source`, and
`fallback_chain`. If evidence is ambiguous, the system can explicitly mark it as unresolved
instead of silently assigning the last-known or document-level ESN.

**Interview signal:** production AI quality and auditability. For high-stakes engineering use
cases, knowing *why* the system believes a piece of evidence belongs to a machine is as
important as having the label itself.

#### 6. Your retrieval evaluation story is stronger than a single recall number

The MLflow evaluation showed why a headline document-recall metric can be misleading. In the
initial sample, document recall was ~94% across configurations, but evidence-level inspection
still found adjacent-page matches, semantically wrong “oil” matches, and one missing expected
report. Increasing top-K beyond 10 added context without demonstrating additional evidence
coverage on that sample.

**Interview signal:** you distinguish “the system found the document” from “the model received
the evidence required to make the right decision.” That is a senior RAG-evaluation mindset.

# 8. How to Answer So You Sound Like a Senior AWS SA

> Do not tell the story as a timeline of everything that happened. Tell
> it as a sequence of decisions you made.

- Start with the customer/business problem in 1–2 sentences.

- State your ownership clearly: “I was responsible for…”, “I decided…”,
  “I recommended…”.

- Spend most of the answer on 2–4 decisions or actions that reveal
  judgment.

- Explain trade-offs, not just implementation. Why this path? What did
  you reject? What customer constraint mattered?

- Use technical detail selectively to prove depth. The detail should
  explain a decision, failure mode, or trade-off.

- Quantify the result and distinguish proven metrics from estimates or
  preliminary evidence.

- End with a short reflection: what you learned, what changed in how the
  customer operated, or how your role/trust expanded.

## The 90-second mental structure

1.  Customer pain / stakes — 15–20 sec

2.  My responsibility — 10 sec

3.  My 2–3 key decisions/actions — 45–60 sec

4.  Measured result + business meaning — 20–30 sec

5.  Reflection — 10 sec

## Language that fits this role

- “I started by understanding the customer constraint rather than
  defending my initial recommendation.”

- “I worked backward from the decision the customer needed to make.”

- “I separated what we knew from what we were assuming, then tested the

## Authentic Growth / Weakness Framing

Your evidence bank contains several real growth themes. For this AWS SA role, the safest and
most credible primary weakness is **tailoring technical depth to the audience**.

Recommended framing:

> “I come from a deep technical background, and earlier I sometimes went too far into
> architecture detail before establishing the decision the audience needed to make. I’ve become
> much more deliberate about starting with the business outcome and trade-off, then changing
> the depth based on whether I’m speaking with executives or engineers. I still keep the
> technical depth, but I use it when it helps the audience make the decision.”

Why this works:
- it is supported by repeated feedback;
- it does not undermine your core technical credibility;
- it shows a concrete change in behavior;
- it directly supports the dual audience expected of an AWS SA.

Good backup growth themes, if a specific question makes them relevant:
- escalating or surfacing delivery risk earlier to the broader team rather than only in 1:1s;
- challenging unrealistic customer expectations earlier and more diplomatically;
- moving from solving the immediate issue to identifying the highest-impact opportunity.

For any weakness answer, spend **less time naming the weakness and more time on the mechanism
you changed**.
